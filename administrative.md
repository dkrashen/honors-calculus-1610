<h3>Administrative details for MATH 1610 — Honors Calculus I (Fall 2026)</h3>

### Course Description
This course is an honors version of MATH 1410, covering the
material in greater depth than that course, with more challenging problems and more attention to definitions and to the reasons behind the results, including proofs. 


Textbook: Tom Apostol, Calculus, Volumes I and II, second edition. On reserve at MPA library, DRL 3N1.
### Course Topics and Textbook Sections

Topics covered:
- The language of set theory
- Rigorous overview of the real and complex number systems
- Limits and continuity
- Differentiation
- Integration
- Vector algebra, vector fields, vector calculus
- Line integrals
- Multiple integrals
- Surface integrals

### Exams
We will have <b>three in-class exams</b>. Each exam will be <b>preceded by
a review day</b> in class. 

There is <b>no separate final exam</b> during the registrar’s final exam period.

### Grading Policy
Your course grade will be based on the following components:

 - <b>In-Class Exams (70%)</b><br />
   There will be three in-class exams. The <b>highest exam score</b> will count for <b>30%</b> of your final grade, and the <b>other two exam scores</b> will count for <b>20% each</b>.<br />
   Exams are cumulative in spirit, with emphasis on material covered since the previous exam. The third exam emphasizes synthesis and later topics, but strong performance on any exam is rewarded.
 - <b>In-Class Work (15%)</b><br />
   Periodic short in-class activities, typically completed in small groups. These activities are designed to support understanding, discussion, and engagement. They are graded primarily on participation and serious effort.
 - <b>Homework / Practice (15%)</b><br />
   Regular homework assignments are intended primarily as practice and preparation for class and exams. Homework is graded for completion and coherence rather than correctness.

Final letter grades will be assigned using a standard grading scale, with adjustments made as appropriate to reflect overall course performance.

### Academic Integrity
Students are expected to uphold the University of Pennsylvania’s standards of academic integrity in <b>all aspects of this course</b>, including homework, in-class work, writing assignments, and exams. You are responsible for ensuring that all work you submit reflects your own understanding and effort.

Course activities are designed to help you learn the material; they are most effective when you actively work through problems yourself, even when using outside help. Seeking assistance from classmates, course materials, or other resources is encouraged where appropriate, but copying solutions or submitting work you do not understand undermines the learning process and is not permitted.

All suspected violations of academic integrity will be handled in accordance with the University’s policies. Students are expected to be familiar with the Penn Academic Integrity Policy, available at:<br />
<a href="https://catalog.upenn.edu/policies/pennbook/academic-integrity/">https://catalog.upenn.edu/policies/pennbook/academic-integrity/</a>
